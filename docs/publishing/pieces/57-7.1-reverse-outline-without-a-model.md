## Outcome

**Built 24 November 2026.** Decided: it shows in the Critique.

**What the writer gets:** in the Critique, select a chapter. Under its stage there is one line, **"Scene outline (N scenes) ▸"**. Click it and each scene is listed with:

- its first words, as a link that opens the chapter with them selected;
- its length in words;
- its share of dialogue;
- who speaks, and how many lines each (lines with no clear speaker show as "unclear").

It is closed by default and stays open or closed while the app runs. It moves with the Critique into its own window (2.5).

**Code:**

- New `src/ai/reverse_outline.py` (no Qt):
  - `split_scenes(text)` is **the one rules-based scene splitter**. A break is a scene-break line (`markdown_editor.SCENE_BREAKS`: `***`, `* * *`, `#` …) or two blank lines in a row;
  - `outline(text, names)` returns `Scene` objects (number, start, end, words, dialogue_words, speakers, unattributed, opening), cached in memory by the text's hash;
  - `describe(scene)` gives the one-line summary.
  - Speakers come from `dialogue_speakers.attribute_lines`; dialogue is the words inside quotes.
- New `src/ui/outline_section.py`: `html(issues, chapter_report, text)` and `install(scorecard)`, which gives the issue list the project's character names.
- `ChapterIssues.show_chapter` keeps its arguments, so the toggle can redraw. The outline reads the live text even when the check is out of date.
- One call line in `scorecard_widget.py`.

**Tests:** new `tests/test_reverse_outline.py` (7 tests). Three scenes are checked against counts worked out by hand in the test, plus the toggle, the links, and that the Whole manuscript row has no outline.

**For 7.2 and 7.3:**

- Use `reverse_outline.split_scenes` and `outline`.
- The model's scenes (`chapter_digest`) carry each scene's first words, so 7.2 can match them to these scenes by `opening`.
- 7.3's plan comparison belongs in the same section.

**Not done:** pacing figures per scene (sentence length and the like). The brief named `pacing_analyzer`, but its output is per chapter and adds little per scene. Easy to add with `pacing_analyzer.analyze_text(part)` if wanted.
