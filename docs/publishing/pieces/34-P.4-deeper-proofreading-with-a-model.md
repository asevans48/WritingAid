## The piece

- **Why:** rule-based grammar checking is where the app is furthest behind. A
  capable model reads for errors better than rules do, but it also rewrites
  things it should leave alone.
- **Build:** a proofreading pass that asks the model for corrections only, then
  keeps a correction only if it is a small change (a few words), leaves the
  rest of the sentence identical, and does not touch dialogue quirks or
  anything in the style sheet. Kept corrections appear as ordinary grammar
  flags with the model named as the source.
- **Read first:** `src/utils/proofing.py` (`merge_issues`), `src/ai/flag_judge.py`.
- **Tiers:** without a model, nothing changes. Local models proofread a
  paragraph at a time.
- **Tests:** on the benchmark, the pass must find errors the rules miss without
  raising the wrong-flag rate above the Q.2 target.
- **Done when:** the Q.2 table shows more seeded errors found with the model
  than without, at the same or better accuracy.

## Outcome

**Status:** done, 23 November 2026, together with four pieces of work the
writer asked for in the same session (below). Tested with stand-in models;
see *Measured* for what has and hasn't been run with a real one.

**Built (P.4):**
- `src/ai/proofread.py`. The model is asked for corrections only, one line
  each (`"wrong words" -> "corrected words" | why`). A correction is kept
  only if (`narrow`, `keep`, `parse`):
  - its words are in the text exactly;
  - at most three words differ (`MAX_CHANGED_WORDS`); the flag covers only
    those words;
  - it isn't only a different style of quote mark or dash;
  - it doesn't change a project name or word (characters, places, the
    dictionary, allowed words);
  - inside dialogue, it fixes a misspelling or a doubled word and nothing
    else.
- `src/utils/proof_store.py`. Kept corrections are stored by the exact
  words of their paragraph (in memory, and in `readiness.json` under
  `"proof"`). A changed paragraph is proofread again; the rest is not sent
  twice. Cloud models read about 1,200 words a request, local ones 350.
- They show as **ordinary grammar flags**: `FlagRules.paragraph_flags`
  merges `proof_store.issues_for(text)` after LanguageTool and the built-in
  rules (rule id `MODEL`, reason "… (found by <model>)"). A rule's flag on
  the same words wins.
- The readiness check "Spelling and grammar left" counts them: when a
  model judges a chapter's copyedit stage, `_judge_chapter` calls
  `proofread.add_findings`. A failed proofread is tried again next time.
- After "Check the book with the model", the open chapter's underlines are
  refreshed (`scorecard_model_run.show_corrections_in_editor`). The
  estimate now allows 6 requests a chapter (12 on a local model).
- Without a model nothing is stored and nothing changes.

**Also built in this session (asked for by the writer, no brief):**
1. **Two false alarms fixed.** An accented word is one word
   (`proofing.SPELL_TOKEN_RX`, `unaccented`): "Ántonia" is no longer cut to
   "ntonia", and "café", "naïve", "Zoë" are known. "I was not" is no longer
   an agreement error.
2. **The book's style sheet** (`src/ai/story_ledger.py`). In the same
   request as the story read, the model lists up to 8 facts the chapter
   establishes, each with the words that establish it (`FACT | name | fact |
   "quote"`; a fact whose words aren't in the chapter is dropped). Each
   later chapter is read against the earlier facts about the people and
   things it mentions (up to 40; 15 for a local model) and the latest
   timeline facts. A finding says where the contradicted words are: "In
   “Chapter 3”: “…”. … Which is intended?"
   - Stored in `readiness.json` under `"ledger"`, per manuscript and
     chapter. Re-reading a chapter keeps the facts whose words are still
     there, so one edit doesn't make every later chapter look changed.
   - The story checks' cache key includes the facts a chapter is read
     against (`CheckSpec.depends`), and `model_work` counts a chapter whose
     earlier facts changed. Their versions went to 2, so every chapter is
     read once more.
   - The writer reads the sheet from the scorecard's **Whole manuscript**
     row ("Show the style sheet": facts by character or thing, timeline
     last; a fact's quote opens its place in Write). No new button.
   - The writer can **reword**, **remove** or **put back** a fact, from
     links beside it (`Ledger.edit`, `remove`, `restore`). A removed fact is
     kept (marked `removed`), so reading the chapter again doesn't bring it
     back. Chapters that were read against a changed fact are read again by
     the next check with the model.
5. **A local model is named by what it loads.** `LLMClient.model` was the
   provider's default name for every local model, so results and logs
   named the wrong one; it is now the configured model's id.
3. **One set of rules for how every Critique report puts a finding**
   (`src/ai/editorial_standards.py`, `with_standards`): passage, effect,
   option; ask where the author may have meant it; keep meaning and voice;
   few points; about the text, not the writer. Three call lines in
   `chapter_analysis_agent.py` (sent, and recorded as sent) and one in
   `passage_critique.py`. Answer formats are unchanged.
4. **Misses are measured** (`scripts/measure_misses.py`,
   `benchmark/story/cases.json`, results in `docs/publishing/miss_rates.md`).
