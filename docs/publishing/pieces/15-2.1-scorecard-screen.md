each later check appears on screen as it is added.

## The piece

- **Where:** the front page of the existing Critique tab. No new top-level tab.
  The detailed critique reports that tab shows today open from a chapter's
  row.
- **Agreed in 0.2:** the Critique tab today has a set-up area (scope,
  reports to run, writing context) and results tabs: 11 report types plus an
  Overdescribed tab ([screens.md](../screens.md), row 12). The scorecard sits
  above these and replaces none of them. Running, rating, saving, loading
  and exporting critiques stay one click away, unchanged. After D.4 the
  Critique sits in the **Revise** group beside Prose Profile, whose targets
  the scorecard can point to.
- **Manuscripts:** a selector at the top switches between the main manuscript
  and each novella, as the editor's manuscript tabs do. Each has its own grid
  and its own figure.
- **The writer gets:** a **Path to publish** view. Chapters run down the side,
  checks run across the top grouped by stage, each cell is coloured and shows a
  count, and one figure at the top gives the book's readiness.
- **Build:** new `src/ui/scorecard_widget.py`, reading `book_report`. Tooltips
  on each column explain the check and its threshold. A plain-words legend. A
  note at the top saying whether the results are a scan or were judged by a
  model.
- **Honest wording:** the figure is labelled "ready for readers", with one line
  beneath it: "This measures the mechanics of the writing. It cannot tell you
  whether the story works; readers and an editor can." The word "publishable"
  is not used for a number anywhere.
- **Layout:** follows the agreement from 0.2 and [look and feel](../look-and-feel.md):
  the stage ribbon across the top, one plain figure for the book, the heat map
  below. The grid scrolls in both directions with the chapter names and the
  check names staying in view. On a narrow window the stages collapse to one
  column each and expand on click.
- **Tiers:** this piece shows scan results only.
- **Tests:** the grid matches a report; an empty project shows a helpful
  message, not an empty grid.
- **Done when:** the view opens on a real project and renders correctly in a
  screenshot at a small and a large window size.

## Outcome

**Status:** done, 23 November 2026. Waiting for the writer to try it on a
real project.

**Built:** `src/ui/scorecard_widget.py`.

- `ScorecardWidget`, at the top of the Critique tab:
  - **Path to publish**, with a manuscript selector (shown when there are
    novellas) and one primary button, **Check readiness**.
  - The figure, **"ready for readers"**, with the agreed line beneath it.
  - A note saying it is a scan, what is left out of the figure and why,
    and whether the results couldn't be saved.
  - The **stage ribbon** (Structure › Scene › Line › Copyedit › Proof).
    Stages with no checks yet are greyed.
  - The **heat map**: chapters down the side, checks across, grouped and
    coloured by stage. Each cell is coloured by status, shows a count, and
    has a tooltip with the rate per 1,000 words and the message. Column
    tooltips explain the check and its threshold.
  - A plain-words legend, and an empty state that says what to do.
- **Checking:** runs in the background (R.2) and re-checks whenever the tab
  is shown. It's quick from the cache, and progress shows in place. The
  open chapter's live text is used.
- **Layout:**
  - The grid scrolls both ways, with chapter and check names staying in
    view. It is as tall as its rows, up to 12.
  - On a narrow window each stage folds into one column (the worst status
    and the total). Clicking a stage in the ribbon opens or folds it.
- **Declutter:** the set-up groups (Scope, Reports to run, Writing Context)
  fold into one **"Detailed critique"** section. It is closed by default and
  remembers whether it was left open. Every control in it is the same
  object, so it behaves the same way.
  - Run Critique, the results tabs, rating, saving, loading and exporting
    are unchanged and still on the page.
  - **Double-clicking a chapter's row** opens the detailed critique set to
    that chapter.
- **Call lines only:** `grader_widget.py` (3 lines: install, and update on
  manuscript changes); `main_window.py` (1 line: the live text source).
- `scripts/screen_harness.py` now waits for background work before
  taking each picture.

**Fix after the writer's first look:** check names spilled out of their
header boxes because the header was sized for regular text but drawn in
bold. The header now uses the bold font for both sizing and drawing.
- All columns share one width, at least wide enough for each name, and
  stretch into the free room up to about twice that.
- Names wrap to the width their column gets, and the header is as tall as
  the longest name.
- The grid is never taller than about a third of the window.
- Very long chapter names are shortened with "…"; the full name is in the
  tooltip.
- Checked with 24 chapters and with 8 checks at every target size.
- The Critique's report checkboxes showed "Tension _Stakes"; the "&" is now
  escaped.

**Added after the writer's second look: what each cell means.**
`src/ui/readiness_issues.py` adds `ChapterIssues`, a panel under the grid.
- Click a chapter to list everything its checks found; click a cell to see
  that check alone, with a link to show every check again.
- Each item shows the passage, with spelling and grammar in their sentence.
  It also gives the reason, the suggested fixes, and **Show in chapter**,
  which opens the chapter in Write with the passage selected
  (`show_passage`).
- Overly descriptive sentences also link to **Ways to expand it…**, the
  existing expand window, through the Critique's own signal.
- A repeated word or sentence is one entry with "×N".
- The panel is as tall as its list, up to about 45% of the window.
- The chosen chapter is marked on its name, so its cells keep their colours.
- **It follows the writing:** each time the tab opens on a different chapter
  in Write, the scorecard switches to that chapter's manuscript (main or
  novella) and shows that chapter. The writer can pick any other chapter or
  manuscript, and the pick stays until Write moves to another chapter.
- **The detailed critique follows the click.** For the chapter open in
  Write it is set to "Current chapter". For any other chapter it is set to
  "Selected chapters" with that chapter ticked in the same picker, which the
  writer can still change. The section is not opened; double-clicking still
  opens it.
- One more call line in `main_window.py` connects Show in chapter.
- Tests: 6 more in `tests/test_scorecard.py`.

**Also at the writer's request: the detailed critique runs in parts** (it
was slow on a local model).
- `src/ai/critique_parts.py` (`run_in_parts`) runs the same reports one at
  a time, through the orchestrator's own `run_chapter`.
- **Results as ready:** each report's tab appears as soon as it is done
  (`src/ui/critique_live.py`). The tab the writer is reading keeps its
  place.
- **Stop:** Run Critique becomes Stop while it runs, so no button is added.
  Stopping keeps the finished reports. It also skips the slow model steps
  that follow (the overly descriptive review falls back to the scan, and the
  promise and continuity checks are skipped). A stopped run is not recorded
  in the critique history.
- **Resume:** each report a model writes is kept in a new per-project file,
  `critique_resume.json` (`src/data/critique_resume.py`, written through
  `safe_store`, redirected in tests).
  - The next run with the same chapter text, report, model, genre and
    writing context reuses it.
  - A completed run empties the file, so a fresh run always gets fresh
    reports.
- The saved-critique format is unchanged. Its section fields now come from
  one place (`section_to_dict` / `section_from_dict`).
- Tests: `tests/test_critique_parts.py` (7).

**Also at the writer's request: findings become records.**
- **World elements:** the Worldbuilding report's new elements have
  **＋ Add or connect…**. It opens a pop-up that either adds a new element
  (draftable by AI) or connects the name to an element or term already in the
  World. A connected term goes in World → Encyclopedia with `refers_to`; it
  then counts as a mention of that element and stops being listed as new.
- **Characters:** the Characters report's unrecorded speakers have
  **＋ Add as a character…**, with the same choice: a new character, or
  **another name for an existing one**. A nickname is written to that
  character's notes as "Also called: …", which the checks, mention-finding
  and spell checker read (`canon_index.character_aliases`).
- **Bug fixed:** Encyclopedia entries made in the World tab (with tags) made
  the World fail validation on save; they are now stored as text.
- Code: `src/ai/canon_from_critique.py`, `src/ui/canon_add_dialog.py`.
  Tests: `tests/test_canon_from_critique.py` (20).
