## Phase context

**The idea.** Ratings stay in one shared store, so everything rated in any
project keeps teaching the app. Each rating is tagged with the voice of the
writing it was given to. Ratings fall into a small number of voice groups.
When the app writes a rewrite, it learns first from ratings in the same voice
group as the passage, whichever project they came from. Two books in different
voices stop colouring each other; two in a similar voice help each other; and
one book with several narrators gets a group for each.

**My reading of "broad, manageable".** Broad: shared across projects, with a
few large groups, not many small ones. Manageable: the writer can see the
groups, rename them, move a rating, merge two groups, and remove ratings.
Correct this in the review notes if you meant something else.

## The piece

- **The writer gets:** a view of ratings grouped by voice: each group's name,
  description, how many ratings it holds and from which projects, and the
  ratings inside it. The writer can rename a group, move a rating, merge two
  groups, change or remove a rating, and rebuild the groups.
- **Where:** inside the screen that already shows rating history (confirm in
  0.2 which that is). No new window or tab (rule 13).
- **Fits rule 12:** the list scrolls, and the view works at 1024 × 600.
- **Done when:** a rating can be found, moved and removed, and a moved rating
  stays where the writer put it after a rebuild.

## Outcome

_Built 25 November 2026._

**Where the writer finds it.** Training Studio ▸ **🔎 Browse rows…** ▸
Source: **rated rewrites, by voice** (the entry just below "rephrase").
That dialog is the one screen that already listed and removed rated rows
(confirmed: the Rephrase window has the rating row but no history; the
writing app's Settings only switch collection on and off). The groups are
a way of sorting its list, not a new window: choosing the entry puts the
tree in place of the table and steps the table's Prev / Next / Drop
matching buttons aside; the Search box finds a rating inside the groups;
any other Source brings the table back exactly as before. No main-window
screen, tab, button or menu changed; counts are the same as before
(`scripts/screen_harness.py`).

**What the writer sees.** One tree, scrolling:
- **Suggestions** from V.2 (join, merge, leave, split, unsplit), one line
  each: 💡 *Accept · Decline* and the suggestion in words. The "more than
  8 voices" merge is shown only if the merged group would stay strong
  (widest pair ≤ `SAME`).
- **Groups**, largest first: the name (or, until V.3 names it, the plain
  description; a writer's name shows as "Name — description"), ratings,
  and how alike with the projects it draws on ("very alike · Teller
  County"). A group holding one source says **"one project"** or **"one
  narrator"**; a group of only example passages says so.
- Inside each group its **projects and narrators** (📁 project, 🎙
  narrator), 📌 when placed by the writer, 🔒 and "kept to itself" when
  the project keeps its ratings to itself.
- Inside each source its **ratings** (loaded when opened): the rewrite,
  the rating, the date; the passage and rewrite in the tooltip.
- **Unsorted** below the groups: sources with too few rated passages
  ("needs 24 more rated passages") and any waiting beyond the 8.
- One line under the tree says how the number of groups comes about
  (`voice_ratings.WHY_COUNT`).

**Actions** (right-click, and the one **Voice groups ▾** menu, which
offers the same for the selection): Rename…; Merge N groups (select two or
more) or Merge with ▸ (one selected); Remove this group (its projects are
sorted again; ratings untouched); Move to ▸ a group or New group…; Let the
app sort it (unpins); Keep this project's ratings to itself (a tick);
Change rating ▸; Remove this rating… (asks; also the Delete key); New
group…; Rebuild the groups; Undo (also Ctrl+Z). Every change except
removing a rating can be taken back until the dialog closes. A merge whose
result would be loose asks first, in the writer's words. **New group…**
takes an optional name and the projects or narrators ticked, and/or
example passages pasted (a line of `---` between them; ones too short to
measure are left out after a warning).
