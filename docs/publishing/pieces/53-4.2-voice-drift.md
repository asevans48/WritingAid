## The piece

- **Prove it first:** before any screen is built, test the measure on real
  chapters: an untouched chapter, one revised by the writer, and one rewritten
  by a model in a plain style. If the measure cannot tell them apart, stop and
  drop this piece.
- **The writer gets:** a book-level check showing how far revised chapters have
  moved from the writer's own voice, with the chapters that moved most.
- **Build:** compare each chapter's voice profile with the book's baseline,
  using `src/ai/writer_voice.py`. A new column on the scorecard.
- **Tiers:** the measure itself needs no model.
- **Tests:** a chapter rewritten in a different style scores as drifted; an
  untouched one does not.
- **Done when:** drift is visible per chapter and a drifting rewrite is marked
  before it can be accepted.

## Outcome

_26 November 2026. **Dropped at the gate.** Nothing was built in the app;
screen counts unchanged._

**Verdict.** The measure the brief names (the writer's voice profile from
`writer_voice.py`, as V.1's 23-number fingerprint) does recognise a
different *human* author, but it cannot tell a chapter a model rewrote from
one the writer wrote. A model's rewrites of the writer's passages sit about
0.25 apart from the originals, while the writer's own chapters (and any
published book's) differ from each other by up to 0.6 or more at chapter
size. A bar high enough to leave untouched chapters alone (0.70) caught none
of the model-rewritten sets. As the brief says, the piece stops here.

**The measure tried.** A chapter is cut into passages of about 200 words at
paragraph ends; each passage gets V.1's fingerprint (`voice_fingerprint`).
A chapter's drift is V.2's separation (`voice_groups.separation`) between
its passages and those of every other chapter of the same manuscript: the
gap between the two means in within-voice spreads, with the part chance
gives taken off (0 = the book's own voice). This is the chance-subtracted
measure V.2 calibrated, used here at chapter size. A rewrite was measured on
the ~200 words around it, before and after, against the book's passages.

**Data (read only).**
- *Published books:* 45 downloaded Gutenberg books (P.3), 1,425 chapters
  (median 9 passages each).
- *The writer, untouched:* every chapter of Born in Blood (6) and of the
  Teller County novella *Rifle Creek* (7, the main manuscript has none),
  from the project paths in the ratings rows; and **47 earlier drafts of
  Born in Blood** (`Adelphus/Adelphus - The Fall/chapters/Ch *.docx`, read
  with python-docx).
- *The writer, revised:* a truly revised chapter was available. Born in
  Blood chapters 1, 2, 3, 5 and 47 are the writer's revisions of those
  earlier drafts (37%, 36%, 4%, 2% and 1% of the draft's words kept), and
  chapter 1 has two revisions of its own (51% of words kept). These may
  include the few rewrites the writer accepted; that is what "revised by
  the writer" means in practice.
- *Rewritten by a model:* no model was run. The ratings database
  (`~/.creativeos/rephrase_history.db`, opened with `mode=ro`) holds **398**
  rated rewrites (not thousands: the other 200,000 rows are corpus, plot
  and chat rows, not rewrites). Used: the 74 plain ones the writer asked for
  (`concise/…`, `clearer/…`, not edited by the writer, not one-word
  suggestions; 36 from the Adelphus books, 38 from Teller County), and 247 a model
  made from Born in Blood passages in a tone (noir, comedic, light, ironic,
  romantic, tragic, grimdark; `synthesized=` rows). Each has its original
  beside it. No whole chapter rewritten by a model exists, so it was
  approximated two ways: the rewrites gathered into chapter-sized sets
  (wholly rewritten), and each rewrite put back in its own ~200 words of
  context, eight to a set (partly rewritten: 15 to 21% of the words the
  model's).

**The numbers** (`venv/bin/python scripts/voice_drift_gate.py --writer
--earlier-drafts "<The Fall chapters>"`, seed 7, 10 s).

How to read it: the model's rewrites move the writer's prose by about 0.25
(tone) to 0.5 (plain, a small sample), with the clearest single signs being
more -ly adverbs (tone) and more dashes and article openings, fewer name
openings (plain). An untouched chapter already strays from its own book by
0.54 one time in ten. So at chapter size the two overlap: a bar of 0.70
misses every model set, and a bar low enough to catch the plain set (0.40)
would mark one untouched chapter in five (21.4% of published chapters).

**The rewrite mark** (the brief's "marked before it can be accepted"),
tried on single rewrites: the passage with the rewrite reads less like the
book than 95% of the book's own passages *and* is 0.35 further away than
before. It marked 10 of 57 model rewrites in context (18%) and 1 of 20 of
the writer's own revised paragraphs (5%); 2 of 2 rewrites whose original is
still in a chapter. That misses four model rewrites in five, so it is not
worth a label either.

**Why it fails.** The app's rewrites are written to match exactly these
measures: `writer_voice.to_prompt` tells the model the writer's sentence
lengths, punctuation habits, contractions, person and tense, and
`check_rewrite`'s voice fit penalises misses. They are made sentence by
sentence and keep the length (length ratio about 1.0). What a model
changes is word choice and imagery, which these 23 measures do not see.
The measure is good at what V.2 uses it for, telling one human voice from
another (74% of other-author chapters caught at 0.70), but not at this.

**A word-based measure, tried for comparison** (section 5 of the script):
rates of 100 common words per passage (Burrows-style), same separation.
Published untouched chapters p95 0.41, p97 0.45; other authors p50 0.55.
Model vs writer: tone 0.28 pooled (writer's halves 0.04), plain 0.44 pooled
(halves 0.00). Plain rewrites would just cross a 0.40 bar (6.3% of untouched
chapters flagged), tone rewrites would not. One plain set is too little to
build on, and it is not the measure the brief named, so it is recorded here
as a lead, not built. See the decision in [checklist.md](../checklist.md)
section 3.
