## The piece

- **The writer gets:** each measure shown against published books in the book's
  genre, not against a fixed number. "Dialogue 31%; published thrillers 35 to
  50%."
- **Build:** extend the baseline the pacing analyser already uses to cover
  sentence length and variety, dialogue share, adverbs, passive voice,
  readability and chapter length. Computed once from the corpora and stored.
- **Read first:** `src/ai/pacing_analyzer.py`, `src/data/conlit_loader.py`.
- **Tiers:** no model. Without the corpus data, the fixed thresholds remain.
- **Done when:** the readiness thresholds come from the genre baseline where one
  exists.

**Where the figures come from, and how many books back each.**
`scripts/genre_baseline.py` (run once by the writer, app closed) measures
the **public-domain Project Gutenberg books already downloaded in the
Training Studio** (`~/.creativeos/corpus_downloads`), the ones each genre
lists in `src/data/genres.py`, with the checks' own code (the editor's
style rules for adverbs and passive, `ProWritingAnalyzer` for sentence
length, variety, dialogue share and Flesch reading ease). Each book is
split into chapters at its headings (500–15,000 words); up to six from
the middle of each book are measured, so every book weighs the same, and
every chapter counts for length. Stored per genre: p10, median and p90 of
each measure, the number of books and chapters, and the titles, in
`~/.creativeos/genre_baseline.json`. Nothing is downloaded.

**How much data there can be.** The genres list at most this many
Gutenberg books: horror 15, romance 13, western 9, adventure 8, scifi 8,
frontier 6, literary 6, gothic 5, mystery 5, thriller 5, **fantasy 3**.

if every listed book is downloaded, and **fantasy can never reach five**
as things stand (it will always say "too little data"). These are books
from the 19th and early 20th centuries, not contemporary ones; the messages say
"published thrillers" and the checklist asks the writer whether the
ranges look right for books like theirs. I did not run the script on the
writer's downloads (the session must not touch `~/.creativeos`), so the
real counts and ranges are not known yet.

**CONLIT for sentence length (24 Nov 2026).** The writer decided "conlit
works": CONLIT now sets sentence length where it has enough books.

- **How CONLIT defines it.** The app reads `avg_sentence_length` from
  `CONLIT_META.csv` (`conlit_loader.load_conlit_metadata`); nothing in the
  repo documents it beyond that column name. Per the dataset's description
  (Piper's CONLIT, Figshare 21166171) the features were made with BookNLP,
  whose tokens are spaCy's: every punctuation mark is a token and "didn't"
  is two ("did", "n't"). So it is tokens per sentence, where the app counts
  words per sentence (`compound_tokens`: letters only, "didn't" and
  "one-half" one word; sentences split at `.!?` and a space). There are no
  per-book sentence or word counts in the file, so no exact conversion is
  possible; this could not be confirmed without the data (the session did
  not open `~/.creativeos`), so the writer's run checks it (below).
- **The conversion.** Measured on the prose already in the repo
  (`benchmark/clean`, 12 public-domain passages; `benchmark/style/marked.json`
  and `benchmark/story/*.json`, modern-written passages; 7,952 words in all),
  counting each text twice: the app's own counter, and spaCy's rule-based
  English tokenizer and sentencizer (`spacy.blank("en")`, no statistical
  model) as the CONLIT-style count. A script in the session's scratch folder,
  reading only those repo files.
  - Tokens per app word: 1.17 overall (1.13 modern prose, 1.20 older prose,
    which has more semicolons and dashes).
  - CONLIT-style tokens per sentence ÷ app words per sentence: **1.12 on the
    modern prose** (56 passages, 3,703 words; 95% by resampling passages
    1.10 to 1.14; 1.13 if both use the app's sentences), 0.99 on the older
    passages (the rule-based splitter breaks at "?” and" where the app does
    not), 1.06 pooled.
  - **Used: CONLIT ÷ 1.12** (`CONLIT_RATIO`), since CONLIT's books are
    contemporary. Likely range 1.05 to 1.20 (`CONLIT_RATIO_RANGE`): BookNLP
    splits sentences with a parser, not rules, and contemporary dialogue has
    more contractions. At 15 tokens a sentence that is 13.4 words, give or
    take about 0.7.
- **Genres.** `conlit_loader.CONLIT_GENRE_MAP` as it stands: MY → mystery,
  SF → scifi, ROM → romance, HIST → literary (historical fiction stands in
  for literary; the message names it). Thriller, horror, western, gothic,
  frontier, adventure and fantasy have no CONLIT code; the other codes (YA,
  bestseller lists, memoir…) are not used. (Whether "PW" means prizewinners,
  a closer match for literary, can only be seen in the data; not changed.)
- **How the sources combine.** Sentence length only: CONLIT's range
  (10th–90th percentile of its books, converted) replaces the Gutenberg
  books' where CONLIT has five or more books for the genre (contemporary
  is closer to the writer's market, and it has many more books). With
  fewer, the Gutenberg figure stays. Both are kept in the genre's
  `sentence_length_sources` for `--show`. A genre with CONLIT but too few
  Gutenberg books (mystery 4, literary 3 on the writer's run) gets CONLIT's
  sentence length and keeps the fixed figures for everything else, saying
  "too little data" for those as before. Every other measure is unchanged.
- **What the writer sees.** Pacing for the genre, for example: "Sentences 11.2 words;
  published mysteries 11 to 16 words (contemporary, CONLIT, 300 books)."
  (literary: "contemporary historical fiction, CONLIT"). The grey note on the
  first check that day: "Sentence length now compared with published
  mysteries (contemporary, CONLIT, 300 books) rather than a fixed figure."
  (or "…; the other measures as before." where the genre already had
  figures). Cached pacing results are recomputed (the figures' fingerprint

- **Stored file.** Gains keys only: top-level `conlit` (books, ratio, books
