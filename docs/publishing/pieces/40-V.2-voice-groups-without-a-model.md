## Phase context

**The idea.** Ratings stay in one shared store, so everything rated in any
project keeps teaching the app. Each rating is tagged with the voice of the
writing it was given to. Ratings fall into a small number of voice groups.
When the app writes a rewrite, it learns first from ratings in the same voice
group as the passage, whichever project they came from. Two books in different
voices stop colouring each other; two in a similar voice help each other; and
one book with several narrators gets a group for each.

**My reading of "broad, manageable".** Broad: shared across projects, with a
few large groups, not many small ones. Manageable: the writer can see the
groups, rename them, move a rating, merge two groups, and remove ratings.
Correct this in the review notes if you meant something else.

## The piece

- **Build:** new `src/ai/voice_groups.py`. It sorts the fingerprints into groups
  by similarity, using the numeric measures only.
  - **Few groups.** The number is chosen from the data and capped at about
    eight. A group needs a minimum number of ratings to exist; smaller
    clusters join their nearest group.
  - **Stable.** Groups keep their identity when new ratings arrive. A rating
    moves group only when the groups are rebuilt, and rebuilding keeps each
    group's name and any ratings the writer placed by hand.
  - **Too few ratings.** Below a minimum total there is one group, and
    everything behaves as it does today.
  - **Described in plain words** from the measures: "short sentences, present
    tense, first person".
- **Dependencies:** numpy is already required. Do not require scikit-learn,
  which the app treats as optional.
- **Tiers:** this piece needs no model.
- **Tests:** passages written in two clearly different voices land in two
  groups; passages in one voice land in one; adding ratings does not rename
  or shuffle existing groups.
- **Done when:** every rating with a fingerprint has a group.

## Review notes

_The writer's changes to this brief, made before the session starts._

- **25 November 2026 (README decision 5): how the groups are set.** This
  replaces "capped at about eight" and the k-means wording in *The piece*.
  The writer asked for a way that is dynamic, strong and manageable, and
  left the method to the session ("square root of n" was an example only).
  The number of groups is never chosen; it is how many strong, distinct
  voices there is evidence for.
  - **Unit: the voice source,** not the single rating. A source is one
    `project_path` + `character_name` (no narrator: the project alone). Its
    fingerprint is the mean of its ratings' standardised measures; its
    spread is their standard deviation. Every rating follows its source's
    group. The writer manages projects and narrators inside groups.
  - **Evidence:** a source counts once it has `MIN_SOURCE_RATINGS` (start
    30) fingerprinted ratings. Until then it is **Unsorted** and its
    ratings are used as today (the whole store).
  - **Separation** between two sources or groups: the distance between
    their fingerprints in units of the pooled within-source spread (so it
    scales itself to the data). `SAME` (start 1.0): at or below, one voice.
    `APART` (start 1.5): at or above, different voices. Between the two,
    things stay as they are (no flip-flopping).
  - **Building:** start with every ready source alone. Merge the closest
    pair of groups (average separation) while it is ≤ `SAME` and no two
    sources in the merged group are ≥ `APART`. Stop when no pair qualifies.
    What is left is the groups. Same data, same groups, every time (no
    random start).
  - **A source with two voices** (its ratings split two ways with
    separation ≥ `APART`, each half ≥ `MIN_SOURCE_RATINGS`) is treated as
    two sources ("<project>: voice A / B").
  - **Strength:** a group's label is from its widest pair of sources:
    "very alike" (≤ `SAME` ÷ 2), "alike" (≤ `SAME`), "loose" (above, only
    possible when the writer merged or placed sources by hand).
  - **Growing and shrinking,** at a check (start-up, when a source became
    ready or the ratings grew by about 20%; or on request): a newly ready
    source joins its nearest group if ≤ `SAME` from it, else starts a group;
    groups ≤ `SAME` apart merge; a source ≥ `APART` from the rest of its
    group leaves it; a source that falls below the evidence goes to
    Unsorted; a group with no source left goes. The larger side keeps the
    group's id and name.
  - **Manageable:** at most `MAX_AUTO_GROUPS = 8` groups made by the app.
    Beyond that the smallest wait in Unsorted and a merge of the closest
    pair is proposed (V.5). Strength comes before the count: the app never
    makes a loose group to reach a number.
  - **The writer's hand:** groups the writer created (seeded with a
    project, a narrator or example passages), named, or placed a source in
    are anchors: never merged, emptied or removed by the app. A change that
    would touch one is stored as a proposal for V.5. Sources placed by hand
    are pinned. The writer may merge any groups (V.5 warns when the result
    is loose).
  - **Events for naming:** record each new or merged group so V.3 can name
    it; until then its name is the plain-words description.
  - **Where the thresholds come from** (25 Nov; 1.0 and 1.5 are only
    placeholders until this is run). Measure two things on text whose
    answer is known:
    - *same voice:* split one source's passages into two random halves and
      measure the separation between the halves, many times. This is how
      far apart two samples of one voice land by chance.
    - *different voices:* the separation between different authors.
    Use the published books already downloaded for the genre baseline (P.3;
    one author per book, passages of the lengths ratings have). `SAME` = the
    95th percentile of the same-voice figures (two samples of one voice
    rarely land further apart). `APART` = the larger of 1.5 × `SAME` and the
    10th percentile of the different-author figures. If the two sets
    overlap heavily the measures cannot tell voices apart: stop and report.
    `MIN_SOURCE_RATINGS` = the smallest sample at which the same-voice
    figure settles (its 95th percentile within a tenth of the large-sample
    value). Keep the script (`scripts/voice_thresholds.py`) and its table
    in *Outcome*. The thresholds are constants from this run; the writer's
    own data does not move them (their groups must not shift because a
    threshold did).
  - **In *Outcome*:** the calibration table, and the separations between
    the writer's real sources beside it as a check.
  - **Tests:** two clearly different voices give two groups, one voice
    gives one; three distinct sources give three groups (no cap forces
    them together); a source between `SAME` and `APART` does not move
    between two rebuilds; a two-voice source becomes two; a source with too
    few ratings is Unsorted; anchors survive every rebuild; never more than
    8 automatic groups; the same data gives the same groups twice.
- "Broad" = shared across projects and "manageable" = the writer manages the
  groups: confirmed.
