## Phase context

Start this phase when the server is running.

## The piece

- **The writer gets:** after a whole-book read, a two-page letter naming the
  three to five most important issues, each with its evidence and links to the
  chapters that show it.
- **Parity with:** ProWritingAid's manuscript analysis.
- **Covers:** the seven elements Editors Canada lists for a manuscript
  evaluation: characterisation, dialogue, setting, plot, structure, voice and
  tone. One letter per manuscript, so a novella gets its own.
- **Tiers:** without a model, the letter is assembled from the scorecard's worst
  results, in the same layout. With a model, it is written from the chapter
  reports and reverse outlines, not from the full text, so it fits any model.
- **Done when:** the letter's points link to real chapters and match the
  scorecard.

## Outcome

**Status:** built 23 November 2026, with the outline and checks it is
written from (parts of 7.2, P.7 and P.8, at the writer's request). Tested
with stand-in models only. **No real model has run any of it**: whether
the letter is worth reading is not yet known. Sources and what they do and
don't support: `background.md`, "What a developmental editor's read rests
on".

**Built:**
1. **A reverse outline of every chapter** (`src/ai/chapter_digest.py`). One
   model request per chapter records its scenes (point of view, what
   happens, what the scene changes or that it changes nothing),
   point-of-view slips, up to three setups and payoffs, how characters
   change, and the reader's pull from 1 to 5 with its weakest place. Quotes
   must be in the chapter. Stored in `readiness.json` under `"digest"` and
   made again only when the chapter changes.
2. **Three chapter checks from the outline**
   (`src/ai/readiness_checks/editor.py`): *Scenes that change nothing*,
   *Point-of-view slips*, *Reader's pull* (a score; a pull of 2 or less in
   the first three chapters is a problem). Two whole-book checks: *Setups
   never paid off* (one model request matching the recorded setups against
   the outline) and *Characters who drop out* (no model: a recorded
   character with 8 or more mentions who never appears in the last third;
   books of 6 chapters or more). Every finding is a question.
3. **The letter** (`src/ai/editorial_letter.py`): `write`, `assemble`,
   `findings`, `parse`, `stored`, `to_html`.
   - With a model: one request, from the outline and the scorecard's
     findings, never the full text. What works; three to five issues, most
     important first, each with its element, the chapters that show it,
     its effect on a reader and one option; what it couldn't judge. An
     issue that names no real chapter, or no known element, is dropped.
   - Without a model, or before the chapters are outlined: assembled from
     the scorecard's worst results in the same layout, and it says so.
   - It always ends with what it can't judge (the prose itself, whole-book
     judgements, acceptance and sales) and that it is one reader's view.
   - Stored under `"letter"`, per manuscript, and marked when the book has
     changed since.
4. **Where:** the scorecard's **Whole manuscript** row: "Write the letter",
   then "Show the letter" and "write it again". A chapter named in the
   letter opens that chapter's row. No new button, tab or section.
5. **Measured** by `scripts/measure_misses.py --model --editor` on
   `benchmark/story/editor_cases.json` (four books with one planted flaw
   each, one with none). Not run with a real model yet.

6. **Before an editor: one ordered pass** (`src/ai/editor_pass.py`, added
   the same day at the writer's request). The aim is that an editor's time
   goes to the book, not to mechanical work. The Whole manuscript row
   shows three steps with where each stands (`plan`):
   1. *Clear the mechanical*: spelling, grammar (with the model's
      proofreading), consistency, the style sheet's rules; with a link to
      the chapter that has most.
   2. *Answer the queries*: what the story read and the outline raised.
      Each query now has **That's intended** (`set_intended`): it stops
      counting, is listed as intended with "take that back", and goes into
      the brief. This is the first use of the dismissals the engine has
      always honoured (`readiness.json`, `"dismissed"`).
   3. *Know the big picture*: the editorial letter.
   "Ready for an editor" means the mechanical work is at a published
   book's level and a model has read every chapter. Open questions don't
   block it: they go to the editor.
7. **The editor's brief** (`editor_pass.brief`; "Save the editor's brief…"
   in the same row, saved as Markdown where the writer chooses). What an
   editor would otherwise build or ask: the state of the manuscript and
   how it was checked, the letter, open questions by chapter, deliberate
   choices (spelling variety, the style rules, everything marked intended),
   the style sheet of characters, places and timeline, and the outline. It
   says it was made by software and that the manuscript wins.

**Cost:** "Check the book with the model" now makes about 7 requests a
chapter (13 on a local model). The letter is one more request.

**Left out, and why:**
- 7.1's outline without a model, and an outline screen: the outline is
  shown only through the checks and the letter.
- 7.2's tension rating (the brief says to prove it repeatable first).
- P.8's section on the Characters screen (speech share, speech
  consistency, arcs): only the drop-out check was built.
- P.7's hook marks as their own row: the reader's pull is a column.
- The writer can't correct an outline line (they can correct the style
  sheet). Research on book summaries says outlines omit key events, so
  this matters; it is the next thing to add.
- The letter's elements differ from the brief's seven: Editors Canada's
  list, read in full, also has believability, and CIEP's has point of
  view.
