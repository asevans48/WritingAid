## Phase context

Phase F makes the app work for stories that are not novels, **keeping every
story feature**: plot, characters, world, critique, the Improve window, Read
Aloud, the Studio. Agreed with the writer on 26 November 2026 (README
decision 18):

- **Kinds.** A project has a kind, chosen when it is created and changeable
  later: **Novel** (novels and novellas, as today, and the default for every
  existing project), **Video series**, **Podcast**, **Editorial series**,
  **Journalism**.
- **Same shape for every kind.** A project still holds a main manuscript,
  novellas and chapters. A kind changes the words on screen (set by the
  kind, editable per project), which checks and goals apply, and what
  publishing prepares. A chapter is one video, one episode or one part.
- **Nothing a novelist has changes.** A Novel project looks and behaves
  exactly as it does before this phase (rule 11). Every test of today's
  behaviour keeps passing unchanged.
- **Phase F comes after the final sweep (9.1).** Sending anything to a site
  (YouTube and others) stays a later integration (decision 7); this phase
  prepares the material.

## The piece

- **Why:** in a true story the characters are real people and the plot
  is what happened, in order.
- **The writer gets**, for Journalism, Editorial series and true-story
  scripts:
  - **People** in place of Characters: the same screen and the same
    analysis (P.8), with the fields a real person needs (role in the
    story, what is on the record, link to their source in F.7) and
    without the fiction-only ones (arc type, invented backstory), which
    are hidden, not deleted.
  - **Timeline** in place of the plot's beats: events with dates, shown
    in order, each linked to the chapters that tell it and to its
    sources. The plot screens that exist are reused; a date is added to
    an event.
  - A check that the telling does not contradict the timeline (dates and
    order), built on the continuity checks that exist.
- **A told story still has a shape.** The story structure tools (acts,
  tension, promises) stay available: a retelling chooses what to reveal
  when. The timeline is what happened; the plan is how it is told.
- **Fiction is untouched:** a Novel project's Characters and Plot are as
  today. A fictional Video series or Podcast keeps Characters and Plot.
  F.0 says how a project says it is a true story (a tick in the
  project's settings, not a sixth kind, unless F.0 decides otherwise).
