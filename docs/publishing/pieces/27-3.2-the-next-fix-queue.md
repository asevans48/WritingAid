## The piece

- **The writer gets:** "Today's three fixes" becomes **Next fixes**. It offers
  the most important open items for the chapter's current stage, one at a
  time, with the suggested rewrites the Companion already produces.
- **Build:** feed `fix_candidates` from the readiness report and filter by
  stage. Keep `Fix`, `FixOption`, Accept and More options… as they are.
- **Presentation:** the **Next fix** card from [look and feel](../look-and-feel.md), with a coloured
  before-and-after and keys for accept, skip and move.
- **A slimmer Companion:** the queue can stand in for the list of three fixes
  and the separate overly descriptive section, since an overly descriptive
  statement is one kind of fix. That would take the sections from seven to
  five: Where you left off, Next fix, Notes, Reader's eye, and Whole book
  (story memory and style sheet together).
- **Trial first:** both existing sections were added recently because they
  helped. In this piece the queue is added and the two sections stay, closed
  by default. They are retired only after you have used the queue on both
  projects and agree. Everything they do must be in the checklist: Check this
  chapter, Expand…, Go to, the suggested rewrites, Accept and More options….
- **Per project:** which sections are open is already remembered per project;
  that record must still be read.
- **Read first:** `fix_candidates`, `three_fixes` and `suggest_fix_options` in
  `src/ai/companion.py`; `find_three_fixes` in `src/ui/companion_panel.py`.
- **Tiers:** unchanged from today: quick fixes without a model, rewrites in the
  writer's voice with one.
- **Tests:** a chapter in the copyedit stage is not offered a structure fix;
  accepting a fix updates the report.
- **Done when:** working through the queue turns the chapter's cells to ready.

- **Data safety:** an accepted fix goes into the chapter through the existing
  call, which checks the passage is still where it was. Notes, the style
  sheet, story memory and the record of which sections are open are read
  from where they are today.
