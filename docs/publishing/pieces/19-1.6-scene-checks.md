## The piece

- **Adds checks:** continuity conflicts, order of events, and whether dialogue
  fits each character's way of speaking.
- **Uses** (corrected in 0.1; two of the four were not what the brief
  assumed):
  - **Dialogue fits the speaker:** `dialogue_craft.speech_profiles(project,
    text)`, then `line_fit(line, speaker, profiles)` for each line from
    `dialogue_speakers.attribute_lines(text, names)`. No model; about 1 ms.
  - **Dialogue problems:** `dialogue_craft.dialogue_issues(text, names)`.
    No model; about 1 ms.
  - **Continuity:** `ContinuityAnalyzer(project, llm).run()` in
    `src/ai/continuity_analysis.py` compares **novellas with each other
    only**. When `has_cross_manuscript_content(project)` is false (a project
    with no novellas), there is nothing to report. Without a model it gives
    the presence map, possible resurrections and unrecorded names; conflicts
    need a model. Continuity *within* one book is the Critique's Canon,
    Characters and Worldbuilding reports, which need a model to go beyond
    counts. Wire those in for the model tier; in the no-AI tier the check
    reports "needs a model".
  - **Order of events:** `src/ai/event_order.py` only checks that a
    *rewrite* keeps its original's order (`order_problems(original,
    rewrite)`). It does not check a chapter against the plan or earlier
    chapters. **There is no order-of-events check to connect.** This piece
    either drops it from scope and lists it as a gap, or compares
    `chapter.planning.events` (with `completed`) against
    `story_planning.freytag_pyramid.events` order as a scan, clearly
    labelled. Decide in review notes before the session starts.
- **Tiers (confirmed in 0.1):** dialogue fit and dialogue problems work with
  no model. Continuity across novellas works partly with no model. Continuity
  within a book needs a model. A check that cannot run without a model
  reports "needs a model" and is left out of the readiness figure in the
  no-AI tier.
- **Done when:** the checks appear and the no-AI report is honest about what it
  could not check.

## Outcome

**Status:** done, 23 November 2026. **Order of events is left out** (a gap,
below).

**Checks** (`src/ai/readiness_checks/scene.py`):

- **Dialogue fits the speaker** (`dialogue_fit`): `attribute_lines`, then
  `line_fit` against `speech_profiles` measured once over the whole manuscript
  (`book_wide`). Not shown until two characters have five or more lines.
- **Dialogue problems** (`dialogue_problems`): `dialogue_issues` (bookisms,
  tag adverbs, names, long turns, lost speakers, exposition, filler, stage
  directions). Not shown for a chapter with no dialogue. On published
  chapters: 92% ready.
- **Continuity in this book** (whole book): `needs_model`, honest about it.
  The Critique's Canon, Characters and Worldbuilding reports do this with a
  model (1.7).
- **Continuity across novellas** (whole book, main manuscript only):
  `ContinuityAnalyzer(project, None)`. Its scan finds possible resurrections
  and shared names with no record, as items; conflicts need a model. Cached
  on every manuscript, not just this one.

**Not done: order of events.** Nothing links a chapter's events
(`chapter.planning.events`) to the plan's events (`freytag_pyramid.events`),
so there is nothing honest to compare; a word-match would be a guess. If you
want it, a later piece could add an optional link from a chapter event to a
plan event.

**Speed and size** (a 102,000-word, 38-chapter novel):
- First check 7.6 s, from the cache 0.25 s.
- After editing one chapter, about 4.8 s. Most of that is the manuscript-wide
  consistency scan, which must look at every chapter; it runs in the
  background.
- `readiness.json` is now written compactly and in batches (every 2 s and at
  the end, instead of after each chapter). It keeps 80 findings per check
  per chapter (25 when ready, 40 when it needs a model; counts stay exact),
  and is about 1.8 MB for that book.
