## The piece

- **Parity with:** ProWritingAid's plot analysis (pacing, structure, missing
  beats).
- **The writer gets:** the reverse outline next to the chapter's planned events,
  with planned events missing from the draft and drafted scenes missing from
  the plan both marked. One chart shows tension across the book.
- **Read first:** `src/ui/outline_panel.py`, `ChapterPlanning.events`.
- **Tiers:** without a model, the chart shows pacing figures instead of tension.
- **Done when:** a missing planned event and an unplanned scene are both visible.

  `outline_section.install`). Tension is rated once per version of a
  chapter, with the outline, when the book is checked with the model, and

  chapters have no scene-break lines, so `split_scenes` finds one scene per
  chapter and tension is rated per chapter. The model's own scenes (1 to 6
  a chapter) show as lines in the outline, not as chart points.

## Outcome

**Built 24 November 2026: the no-model tier only.** 7.2 (the outline with a
model) is not built and no model was run, so the chart shows pacing, not
tension, and matching is by words. A hook is left for 7.2 (below).

**What the writer sees.** In the Critique, select a chapter. The closed line
now reads, for a chapter with planned events, "Scene outline (6 scenes) ▸ ·
1 planned event not drafted · 1 scene not in the plan". Opened:

- under each scene (7.1's first words, length, dialogue, speakers), either
  "Plan: <event> (suggested) · change" or, in amber, "Not in the plan (no
  planned event shares its words) · change";
- after the scenes, "Planned, not found in the draft (N):" with each event,
  or "Every planned event has a scene.", and one faint line saying the match
  is by shared names and words, so check it;
- one small chart: every scene of the manuscript left to right, chapter by
  chapter, a bar for its words (the chapter shown in colour, the rest grey)
  and a line for its dialogue share. The caption says "Without a model this
  shows pacing, not tension."
- a chapter with no planned events says so ("Add them in the chapter's
  outline in the side panel") and still shows the chart.

**Corrections.** "change" beside a scene opens a short menu: every planned
event (ticked when the scene holds it; click to add or remove), "Not in the
plan", and "Use the suggestion" (only when the writer has chosen). A choice
shows as "(yours)", takes that event away from any other scene's
suggestion, and is kept in the readiness file. No new button, tab, window or
section: links and a menu inside the existing collapsed section, so it moves
with the Critique into its own window (2.5). Harness counts for the Critique
are unchanged (6 buttons, 2 sections).

**How it matches** (`plan_match.compare`): words of the event's text and
description, minus common and planning words ("beat", "introduce", "reveal"
…) and a "Beat 1:" prefix, roughly stemmed (arrives / arrived / arrival
meet). A word weighs more the fewer of the chapter's scenes use it; a name
weighs double. An event goes to the scene holding the largest share of its
weight if that share is at least 0.3 and at least one shared word is in
fewer than all scenes **and is more than a character's name** (characters
recur; without this rule "Tobin burns the letter" matched a scene that only
mentions Tobin). A close call leans to the scene in the same place in the
order. A scene may hold several events.

**Precision on a realistic fixture** (`tests/test_plan_beside_draft.py`, a
six-scene chapter of about 400 words, six planned events, one undrafted and
one unplanned scene):
- plan worded close to the draft: 5 of 5 drafted events matched, no wrong
  match; the missing event and the unplanned scene are both marked;
- the same plan with each event left out in turn, in order and reversed (12
  plans, 50 drafted events to find): 50 right, 0 wrong;
- a plan paraphrased away from the draft ("our heroine returns to town by
  boat"): 3 of 5 matched, 0 wrong. Recall falls before precision does, so a
  writer whose plans are worded differently sees more "not drafted" and
  corrects them. Two real projects will tell more (checklist section 1).

**Code** (all new modules small; one call line each in the existing files):

**Left out, and why:**
- **Tension.** Needs 7.2's model reading. **Hook for 7.2:**
  `plan_match.set_tension_source(fn)`; `fn(chapter_id, text, spans)` returns
  one 0–1 value per scene span or `None`. When it gives values,
  `ScenePoint.tension` is filled and the chart draws tension as its line
  (caption "tension, from the model") with no other change.
- **Matching by meaning.** Without a model only shared words count; 7.2's
  scene reading could improve it, with the writer's corrections still
  winning.
- A correction is keyed by the scene's first eight words: if the writer
  rewrites a scene's opening, that scene goes back to the suggestion. Said
  here rather than hidden; storing by position would go wrong whenever a
  scene is added above.
- The plan is `planning.events` only (the outline panel's beats are synced
  into it by the main window), not `scene_list`.
