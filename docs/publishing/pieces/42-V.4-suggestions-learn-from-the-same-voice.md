## Phase context

**The idea.** Ratings stay in one shared store, so everything rated in any
project keeps teaching the app. Each rating is tagged with the voice of the
writing it was given to. Ratings fall into a small number of voice groups.
When the app writes a rewrite, it learns first from ratings in the same voice
group as the passage, whichever project they came from. Two books in different
voices stop colouring each other; two in a similar voice help each other; and
one book with several narrators gets a group for each.

**My reading of "broad, manageable".** Broad: shared across projects, with a
few large groups, not many small ones. Manageable: the writer can see the
groups, rename them, move a rating, merge two groups, and remove ratings.
Correct this in the review notes if you meant something else.

## The piece

- **Build:** when Suggestions, Rephrase, Expand or the Companion's fixes choose
  rated examples, work out the voice group of the passage being rewritten and
  prefer examples from that group. Within a group, this project's ratings
  still come first, as they do today. If the group has too few examples, the
  nearest group fills in.
- **Read first:** `examples_prompt` in `src/ai/rewrite_quality.py`,
  `rated_examples` in `src/data/rephrase_database.py`.
- **Prove it first:** on the benchmark and on sample chapters, compare the voice
  fit of rewrites made with same-group examples against today's choice. Keep
  the change only if voice fit is the same or better.
- **Backwards compatible:** with one group, the examples chosen are exactly
  today's.
- **Tests:** with two voice groups, a passage in one voice is never given the
  other voice's examples while its own group has enough.
- **Done when:** the comparison is written down and the examples follow the
  voice group.

## Outcome

_Built 25 November 2026._

**What was built.** Every place that rewrites with rated examples
(Suggestions, Rephrase, Expand, the Companion's fixes, the next-fix queue
through `companion.suggest_fix_options`, the Companion's taste block) goes
through `rewrite_quality.examples_prompt`, which now asks the new
`voice_examples.choose`. No model; the ratings database and the groups
file are only read.

**How the examples are chosen.**
1. Today's pool as before (`rated_examples`: 40 most recent of each kind,
   this project's first), minus any other project's ratings kept to
   itself.
2. Fewer than two voice groups (`MIN_GROUPS = 2`), the passage's source
   Unsorted (`voice_groups_store.group_for` → None), no groups file, or
   any error: **exactly today's examples and prompt text** (tests compare
   against a copy of the old function).
3. Otherwise the passage's group (its project + narrator; the fingerprint
   of the passage with up to 6,000 characters each side picks the part of a
   two-voice source): its ratings ordered "excellent" first and this
   project next, as today; inside each of those tiers, closest to the
   passage's fingerprint first; then closest wording. One example per
   rated passage.
4. Fewer than asked (2 chosen, 1 rejected, counted separately): the
   nearest group fills in (groups ordered by distance from the passage's
   fingerprint to each group's centre), then today's pool.
- **Pure closest-first was measured and not kept as the first key**: it
  scored a little lower on voice fit than today (0.871 vs 0.885). The
  tiers order is the same or better on every line (0.894). See
  [voice_examples.md](../voice_examples.md). The order is the constant
  `voice_examples.WITHIN_GROUP` ("tiers", "closest", "today").
- The prompt header says "in the voice of this passage" when the group
  chose; otherwise it is today's header.

**Keep this project's ratings to itself.** New `src/data/ratings_privacy.py`
(shared with V.5, which already uses it): `is_private(project_path) -> bool`,
`set_private(project_path, value) -> bool` (either takes a path string or a

### Addendum, 26 November 2026: the trial with the model

**What was run.** `scripts/voice_examples_model_trial.py` (new; it reuses
`voice_examples_compare.py`'s set-up: the ratings database opened
`mode=ro`, rated rewrites copied, fingerprinted and grouped in a scratch
folder; nothing written to `~/.creativeos`). The model was the on-device
`mlx-community/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it-4bit`, with the app closed. For each
passage both arms used the app's own `rewrite_quality.examples_prompt`
(2 chosen, 1 rejected, the passage's own ratings left out) and the app's
own `RephrasingAgent.rephrase`: the same text, surroundings, two styles
(the rating's own style, then "clearer"/"concise"), neutral tone, the
voice measured around the passage, temperature 0.7 and the same seed.
Only the examples block differed. Other blocks Rephrase adds from the
open window (memory, order and dialogue guidance) were left out in both
arms. Each rewrite was scored with `WriterVoice.fit_score` against the
voice measured around the passage.

**Sample.** 16 passages (8 from each of the two groups) of the 80
grouped rated passages, drawn at random from the 75 whose examples differ
between the two choices; 2 rewrites per passage per arm (64 rewrites).
About 4 to 9 seconds a request.

| arm | passages | rewrites | mean voice fit | examples from the passage's own group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today | 16 | 32 | 0.912 | 31 of 47 (66%) |
| V.4 (by voice group, "tiers") | 16 | 32 | 0.919 | 39 of 47 (83%) |

(V.4 is not 100%: the first-person group offers no rejected rewrites, so
its rejected example comes from the nearest group, as designed.)

**Paired, passage by passage:** V.4 better on 6, the same (within 0.005)
on 5, worse on 5; mean difference **+0.007** (standard error 0.007; 95%
bootstrap interval −0.003 to +0.021; sign-flip test p ≈ 0.32). By group:
third person (g1) +0.016, first person (g2) −0.001. One passage gives most
of the gain (+0.095: the model fixed the typo and kept the writer's
two-sentence rhythm); without it the mean difference is about +0.001.

**How far to trust it.** Not far. +0.007 over 16 pairs is well inside
chance: the interval includes zero and a difference this size would turn
up about one time in three with no real effect. What the trial does show
is that V.4 **does no harm** on voice fit with a real model (the gate the
brief set: "the same or better"), while it does what it was built to do:
the examples come from the passage's own voice 83% of the time instead
of 66%. `fit_score` is coarse (rhythm, punctuation, person and tense), so
a small taste effect would not show in it. Telling a real gain from none
would need about 100 or more paired passages, or the writer's own ratings
of blind pairs.

**Side by side** (first option of each arm, short excerpts):

- *Third person, V.4's best case* (fit 0.86 → 0.955). Original: "He reachd
  into the breast pocket of his canvas jacket, removing a triangular
  stone, spinning it by the hole in the center." Today: "He pulled a
  triangular stone from his canvas pocket, spinning it by its center
  hole." V.4: "He reached into his canvas jacket and pulled out a
  triangular stone. He spun it through the hole in its center."
- *First person, a small gain* (0.93 → 0.95). Original: "…Aisle four,
  peroxide next to the bandages." Today: "Aisle four held the peroxide
  next to the bandages." V.4: "I needed aisle four, where the peroxide
  sat next to the bandages."
- *First person, a small loss* (0.965 → 0.945). Original: "Last night's
  warm beer lay next to the mattress. I cracked it open…" Today: "…I
  cracked the can and took a long, frothy swig." V.4: "…I cracked it and
  took a long, frothy swig." (Almost the same rewrite: in most pairs the
  two arms' rewrites differ by a few words.)

**Note on the no-model comparison.** Run again on 26 November on the
current ratings (161 fingerprinted, 80 grouped passages) it gives today
0.888 and "tiers" 0.884 (it was 0.885 and 0.894 on 25 November); the
store has changed since, and both runs are within the same small range.

**Decision.** No change to app behaviour. Results and every rewrite:
the scratch folder's `job1/trial/results.jsonl` (not kept in the repo).
