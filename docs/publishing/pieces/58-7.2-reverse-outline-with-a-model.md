## The piece

- **Prove it first:** run the tension rating twice on the same chapters. If the
  two runs disagree often, show the scene summaries and leave tension out.
- **Adds:** for each scene, what happens, whose point of view it is, what the
  scene is for, and a tension rating. One call per chapter, cached with the
  chapter hash.
- **Also adds two readiness checks:** scenes with no clear purpose, and point of
  view slips. These fill gaps noted in 0.1.
- **Done when:** the outline shows the model's reading and the two checks appear
  on the scorecard.

### The proof: is the tension rating repeatable?

Run with the app closed, on copies of *Teller County* (the novella *Rifle
Creek*, 8 chapters with text) and *Born in Blood* (5 chapters), with the
on-device model `mlx-community/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it-4bit` (the
`local_model_id`), by `scripts/tension_repeat_trial.py`. Each chapter was
rated twice with the app's own prompt (`scene_tension.rate_chapter`), each
run with a different seed. The writer's chapters have no scene-break
lines, so each is one scene to the rules splitter; to test the prompt on
several scenes too, consecutive chapters were also joined in pairs with a
`* * *` break (6 two-scene items).

**The bar, set before the runs:** tension is shown only if at least 90%
of scenes are rated within one point of each other across the two runs
**and** quadratic-weighted kappa is at least 0.6 (substantial agreement
beyond chance; it guards against a model that says 3 for everything).

| run | items | scenes | exact | within one | weighted kappa |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| app's temperature (0.2): Born in Blood | 5 | 5 | 100% | 100% | 1.00 |
| app's temperature (0.2): Teller County | 8 | 8 | 100% | 100% | 1.00 |
| app's temperature (0.2): joined pairs | 6 | 12 | 100% | 100% | 1.00 |
| **app's temperature (0.2): all** | **19** | **25** | **100%** | **100%** | **1.00** |
| stricter, temperature 0.7: all | 19 | 25 | 92% | 100% | 0.91 |

Ratings used 2 to 5. **Bar met at both temperatures.** No run failed to
parse. About 2 to 6 seconds a request.

**What this does and does not prove.** It proves the rating repeats. It
does not prove it is right, and it is relative: rated next to the
chapter before or after it, 4 of 12 scenes got a different number from
the one they got alone (Born in Blood ch. 2: 3 alone, 5 after ch. 1;
*A Chance Occurrence*: 3 alone, 2 beside ch. 2). So a value compares
scenes within one chapter better than across chapters. The chart still
draws one line across the book, and its caption says it is the model's.

| chapter | tension | the model's reason |
|---|---|---|
| Rifle Creek 1, A Chance Occurrence | 3 | atmospheric dread and financial instability |
| 3, Of Men and Monsters | 4 | escalating physical threat and a narrow escape |
| 5, Mental State | 4 | conflict between Danny and his ex escalates |
| 8, Acceptance | 2 | background stress from a missing employee |
| Born in Blood 1, Prey | 4 | survival against predators, immediate injury |
| 4, The Levee Breaks | 3 | political manoeuvring and resource conflict |

### The model's reading on the writer's chapters

The same session made each chapter's outline (`chapter_digest`, 4 to 13
seconds a chapter). The model found 1 to 6 scenes a chapter (rules: 1). For
example, *Allen White Dies in the Beginning*: "Samuel wakes to news of a
neighbor's death… → establishes the death of Allen White"; "Samuel visits
the crime scene and finds the body → reveals the gruesome nature of the
death"; six scenes in all. Point-of-view slips: one, in *Crossroads*.

Two problems found in the real outlines:
- **The model copied the line's field names back** as a scene
  (`SCENE | POV | what happens | what the scene changes …`) in 3 of 13
  chapters. Fixed: `chapter_digest.is_template_echo` drops such a line when
  parsing, and the outline skips one already stored (no version change,
  so no chapter is outlined again).
- **"Scenes that change nothing" over-flags on this model.** In *Prey*
  all four scenes came back "changes nothing" (Dane escapes a predator,
  wakes in a hospital, escapes it, is confronted by a deputy). The check
  puts each as a question, but on this model it is noise. Not changed
  here; a decision for the writer (below).

**Why Cydonia's tension can't be read.** On a chapter that is one scene
to the rules (all of the writer's chapters), it repeats the answer
template and then rates five or six beats of its own invention, in rows
like `1 | 1 | 3 | why` without the `TENSION` prefix, until it runs out of
tokens. It does not rate the one numbered scene. On the two-scene pairs it
mostly answers correctly (both runs readable for 4 of the 6 pairs at 0.2). As a diagnostic only, not what
the app does, the highest value in its own rows repeats well (0.2: 89%
exact, kappa 0.92 on 9 chapters; 0.7: kappa 0.72). Its judgement is
steady, but its answer doesn't follow the format.

**Its outline reads better.** It found finer scenes and no fake ones. Its
five "changes nothing" scenes are plausible quiet beats ("wakes up, gets
dressed, leaves for work"; "Curtis watches the sunset"). In *Prey* it gave
each scene a purpose ("Dane is now a fugitive in Salvation"). Gemma's
four "changes nothing" in *Prey* are Gemma's misreading, not the check's.

**What the app does today with Cydonia** (unchanged). Each tension
request comes back unreadable. Nothing is stored. The outline shows no
tension lines, and the chart shows pacing with its "without a model"
caption. Every check is unaffected. **But the unreadable request is made
again on every check with the model** (about 19 seconds a chapter, about
4 minutes for these 13 chapters), because only a readable result is
stored.

2. **Which model the Critique uses:**

   - Cydonia reads scenes better (finer scenes, no fake ones, no false

   - Rule 2 allows no per-request model setting, so the Critique's one

### Addendum, 27 November 2026: a firmer prompt, and "couldn't be read" stored

The writer's answers: stop the wasted request; tension matters more, but
the Critique's model stays the writer's choice in Settings; try a firmer
prompt and prove it again on both models. Built after 7.4 (scene
markers), on its version of `scene_tension.py`.

**1. "Couldn't be read" is stored** (`scene_tension.ensure`). When the
model answers but the ratings can't be read (`scene_tension.Unreadable`),
the chapter's entry under `"tension"` becomes
`{"unreadable": true, "model", "prompt", "text_hash", "v", "scenes",
"marks" (only with markers)}`. That model is not asked again until the
chapter's text, its markers or the model changes. Another model chosen in
Settings is asked fresh (`model_id(llm)`), and so is any model once the
prompt changes (`PROMPT = 2`). Nothing is shown: `stored` skips such an
entry, so the outline has no tension line and the chart draws pacing, as
before. A model that fails to answer at all (an "Error generating text"
reply, an empty chapter) is not stored, and is asked again next time.
Older entries (no `"prompt"` key) stay valid ratings.

**2. The firmer prompt** (`scene_tension.ask(n)`):
- it says how many scenes there are;
- "Rate each numbered scene above as a whole… Do not split a scene into
  parts";
- one two-line example (`TENSION | 1 | 2 | a quiet supper…`,
  `TENSION | 2 | 4 | the stranger draws a knife`);
- "Answer with exactly N lines";
- it ends "Begin your answer with: TENSION | 1 |".

The app's `generate_text` has no assistant pre-fill, so the start of the
line is asked for in words. Room for the answer is now 120 + 40 tokens per
scene (was 80 + 40).

**The parser** reads `TENSION | n | v | why` lines first. Only when there
are none does it read bare rows (Cydonia's old form), and only when they
are clearly one per numbered scene: exactly N rows numbered 1 to N in
order, as `n | v | why`, or as `n | n | v | why` with the number repeated.
Rows for beats the model split a scene into, out-of-order rows, and
`1 | 3 | 2` (which number is the rating?) are never guessed at: they give
no ratings.

**3. The proof again**, on the same copies, chapters and pairs, with the
same bar plus the ≥ 90% readable rule. App closed, one model process at a
time: Gemma first, then Cydonia through the Critique's loading path
(`build_critique_llm` → `model_for_plot`).
