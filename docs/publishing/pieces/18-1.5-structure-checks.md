## The piece

- **Adds checks:** pacing against the genre, and promises and subplots touched.
- **Adds information, not a check:** chapter length and manuscript length beside
  what is typical. Shown on the scorecard without a colour and left out of
  the readiness figure.
- **Uses** (corrected in 0.1):
  - `publishing_goal` from 1.1.
  - **Pacing against the genre:** the Critique's built-in ranges, which need
    no download: `resolve_genre_profile(project.prose_profile.genre)` →
    `GenreProfile` in `src/ai/chapter_analysis_agent.py` (11 genres plus
    a default), compared with the numbers `PacingAnalyzer` computes in its
    `_compute` step. That step needs no model.
    `pacing_analyzer.compare_to_genre` is an extra, used only when
    `conlit_loader.get_genre_stats_cached()` returns data. It is `None` in a
    fresh install, because the CONLIT data is a Training Studio download.
  - **Subplots touched:** `chapter.planning.subplot_notes[].subplot_id`
    against `story_planning.subplots`. No model needed.
  - **Promises touched:** nothing links a chapter to a promise
    (`story_planning.promises`). With a model, `PromiseChecker` relates
    them. Without one, the check reports "needs a model" and is left out
    of the readiness figure. A word-match heuristic would be a guess, and
    rule 4 says to call it a scan if it is used.
- **Book-level results:** subplots that no chapter touches (no model), and
  promises that no chapter touches (model only).
- **Tiers:** with no genre given, or one `resolve_genre_profile` does not
  know, the pacing check uses the default profile and says so. It never
  reports "no baseline" just because the CONLIT data is missing.
- **Done when:** the checks appear, with the book-level result and the length
  information.

## Outcome

**Status:** done, 23 November 2026.

**Checks** (`src/ai/readiness_checks/structure.py`):

- **Pacing for the genre** (`pacing`): `PacingAnalyzer._compute` against
  `resolve_genre_profile(prose_profile.genre)`, no model and no CONLIT data.
  Each "Risk:" finding is an item. Published chapters usually stray on one
  measure, so one is ready, two need work, three are a problem. With no
  genre, or one the app has no figures for, it uses the general profile and
  says so.
- **Chapter length** (information): the words beside the common
  2,000–5,000. Shown without a colour, never in the figure.
- **Whole-book checks** (new in the engine, see below):
  - **Manuscript length** (information): 1.1's length note.
  - **Subplots touched**: plan subplots (not abandoned or resolved) that no
    chapter's subplot notes link to. Until any chapter links a subplot, it
    says so instead of flagging them all.
  - **Promises kept**: `needs_model` (nothing links chapters to promises), shown
    only when the plan has promises.

**Engine additions** (`readiness.py`; the 1.2a design anticipated them):
- `level="book"` checks run after the chapters, with a `BookContext`, cached
  on every chapter's text and the plan (`readiness_state.store_book`).
- A `Book` object shares the manuscript's texts and cross-chapter scans per
  run.
- A check can return `NOT_APPLICABLE` (no cell), set `display="measure"`
  (the cell shows a score), or be `book_wide`.
- The scorecard shows whole-book checks on a last **Whole manuscript** row.
  Clicking it lists what they found, chapter by chapter.
- Information cells show their figure with no colour.
