## Phase context

Nothing in the app says how often a check is right. This phase builds that
measure. Every later claim about quality rests on it.

## The piece

- **Build:** a script that runs every check over the benchmark and reports, per
  check: how many real problems it found, how many it missed, and how many of
  its flags were wrong. It also counts flags per 1,000 words on the clean
  published prose.
- **Output:** a table saved in `docs/`, so each later piece can show a before
  and after.
- **Targets to agree:** a check is shown by default only if at least four in
  five of its flags are right, and the clean prose draws no more than a few
  flags per 1,000 words. Adjust these after seeing the first numbers.
- **Tiers:** runs without a model; an option repeats the run with the configured
  model judging, to show how much the model improves each check.
- **Done when:** the first table exists and the worst checks are named.

## Outcome

**Status:** done. The first table exists and names the worst checks. The
targets stay provisional until the writer decides (open decision 3).

**Built:** `scripts/score_checks.py`. It runs the checks **through a real,
hidden editor**, so every setting matches the app: the quick rules for
dialogue and names, overuse's review of light repeats, and the spell
checker's handling of names. It gives each passage the names a project
would record (`story_names`). Options:

- `--languagetool`: the app's full grammar checking;
- `--model`: repeats the run with the configured model judging the flags,
  through the same `flag_judge` cache the editor uses (in memory only);
- `--no-write`.

It writes [check_scores.md](../check_scores.md) and `check_scores.json`,
for before-and-after comparisons, in about 6 seconds.

**First table, 23 November 2026, with LanguageTool:**

|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar | 85% | 93% | 1.7 |
| Weak intensifiers | 100% | 100% | 2.6 |
| Wordy phrases | 80% | 100% | 1.2 |
| Hard-to-read sentences | 100% | 50% | 2.9 |
| Clichés | 75% | 100% | 0.0 |
| Spelling | 39% | 100% | 5.7 |
| Passive voice | 50% | 100% | 2.4 |
| Repeated sentence starts | 50% | 100% | 0.0 |
| Long sentences | 50% | 100% | 5.0 |
| Overly descriptive | 57% | 100% | 8.1 |
| Adverbs | 50% | 100% | 10.7 |
| Echoes | 20% | 50% | 5.5 |
| Repeated phrases | — | — | 10.0 |
| Overused words | — | — | 22.3 |
| **Sticky sentences** | **6%** | 100% | **34.4** |

**The worst checks:** sticky sentences, overused words, adverbs and echoes
(P.5 is where they get fixed or hidden). Without LanguageTool, grammar
finds only 3 of 12 confused words; with it, 9 of 12.

**What the spelling number means:** most of its wrong flags are period
spellings ("bloweth", "parlourmaid"), and a real bug: **names with
accented letters are split at the accent**. "Françoise" is checked as
"Fran" and "ois", and "Ántonia" as "ntonia". That bug is worth fixing in
P.5, as it hits any writer with accented names.

**Not done:** the `--model` run against a real model. It would send the
benchmark to the configured model, which is slow when the model is local
and costs money when it is in the cloud. The path is tested with a stand-in
model. Run it when convenient:

    venv/bin/python scripts/score_checks.py --languagetool --model

**Targets (open decision 3), for the writer:** taken strictly, "on by
default only if 4 in 5 flags are right" would turn spelling and overuse
off by default. That is not recommended. Spelling's numbers come mostly
from period text and the accent bug, and these are the checks writers
expect. The suggestion is to keep the bar for *style* checks (all off by
default today), and judge spelling and overuse by their clean-prose rate
once P.5 has fixed their worst causes.

**For R.4:** the table's last section compares the two style analysers on
the measures they share:

- *passive*: `ProWritingAnalyzer` is better;
- *adverbs* and *echoes*: `style_checks` is better;
- *clichés*: a tie;
- *sticky sentences*: both poor.
